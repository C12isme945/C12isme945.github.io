PolyVAN-Agent: Repurposing Autoregressive Attention as a Variational Partition Function Solver for Polyelectrolyte–Peptide Assembly & Formulation Design

Yansong Li · South China University of Technology (SCUT) · Code, Data & Interactive Web Workbench: PolyVAN-OSI-Hackathon-2026
Abstract — In functional soft-matter formulations—from biomedical lipopeptide–hyaluronan (HA) carriers (3 ppm Pal-KTTKS) to industrial papermaking wet-end retention aids (0.5–5 ppm cationic PAM)—total additive dosage systematically diverges from active species concentration. At micromolar operating concentrations, all-atom molecular dynamics faces a severe "concentration chasm" (>700 nm simulation boxes), while classical 1D nearest-neighbor transfer matrices (L0 DP) fail when semi-flexible chains form spatial loops that couple sequence-distant hydrophobic ligands. Here we present PolyVAN-Agent, an open scientific intelligence framework that (1) exploits the exact mathematical isomorphism between GPT causal autoregressive decomposition and statistical-mechanical Boltzmann distributions to solve loop-coupled polyelectrolyte partition functions without training data, and (2) embeds this solver as a verified tool inside an LLM experimental-design agent. Against exact 220 (1,048,576-state) enumeration, our causal Transformer achieves 0.00% free-energy error (0.0001 kBT) and importance-sampling ESS = 1.00, proving that ignoring long-range loops underestimates site occupancy by 13.8%. Across a 200-sample prior ensemble, PolyVAN-Agent discovers a sharp oligosaccharide geometric cliff at dp20 (4.0 kDa) and a 150 mM salt-induced cluster reset, enabling automated design of falsifiable ITC/fluorescence protocols (E1–E5) and salt-switchable formulations.

1. Physical Bottleneck: Loops Break Transfer Matrices

Polyelectrolyte–amphiphile formulations operate at trace dosages (3 ppm ≈ 3.74 μM Pal-KTTKS on 0.1 wt% HA), where functional efficacy depends on equilibrium partitioning among four states: free monomer, solution self-aggregate, isolated chain-bound monomer, and chain-templated multivalent clusters.

While 1D dynamic programming (L0 transfer matrix) captures nearest-neighbor cooperativity (ω) and finite chain length (N), HA is a semi-flexible worm-like chain (persistence length lp ≈ 5 nm). Sequence-distant sites |i − j| ≥ 3 contact via spatial looping with probability Ploop(|i − j|) ∝ |i − j|−3/2. Because C16 palmitoyl tails carry strong hydrophobic association (ΔG ≈ −15 to −25 kJ/mol, Boltzmann factor 4.2×102–2.4×104), non-local loop couplings dominate the partition function Z, causing 1D transfer matrices to break down.

2. Isomorphism: GPT Attention as Physics Solver

A lattice polymer with site occupancies s = (s1, …, sN) ∈ {0, 1}N obeys the exact autoregressive probability decomposition:

qθ(s) = ∏i=1..N qθ(si | s1, …, si−1)  ⟺  Causal GPT Token Generation

Rather than fitting external datasets, we train a 2-layer causal Transformer (MiniGPT, d = 48, h = 4) directly on the loop-coupled Hamiltonian E(s) = −μ∑si − J∑sisi+1 − Jlong∑j−i≥3|i−j|−3/2sisj by minimizing the variational free energy upper bound via β-annealed REINFORCE:

Fq = 𝔼s∼qθ[ E(s) + kBT ln qθ(s) ] ≥ Fexact = −kBT ln Z

Causal self-attention naturally captures all-to-all long-range site couplings while supporting exact 𝒪(N) independent microstate sampling and unbiased importance-sampling reweighting (FIS).

4. Physical Discovery: dp20 Cliff & Salt Reset

Coupling our partition solver with a 200-sample prior sensitivity ensemble over binding stoichiometry (nsite ∈ {1, 2, 3} disaccharides/peptide) and minimum nucleus size (nnuc ∈ {3..8}) reveals two falsifiable physical rules:

Fig 1. (Left) Sharp geometric step at dp = 2·nsite·nnuc = 20 (4.01 kDa): effective CAC drops from >285 ppm (dp18) to 2.32 ppm (dp20). (Right) Counterion-release salt switch (I = 1→150 mM): 73.5% templated clusters at 10 mM dissociate into >99% free monomer at 150 mM.
  • Geometric Oligosaccharide Cliff (E2): Across 168/200 templating priors, P(MW90 < 20 kDa) = 97.6% (median 6.4 kDa). Chains shorter than nsite × nnuc disaccharides cannot host a nucleus, creating a 100-fold CAC drop between dp18 and dp20.
